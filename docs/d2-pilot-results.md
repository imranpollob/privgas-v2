## 1. Question and scope

> Can an ERC-4337 UserOperation that is valid when its proof is created, and when a bundler
> simulates it, become invalid before inclusion because an unrelated operation changes shared
> protocol state?

For B3 the shared mutable state is `CreditPaymaster`'s mirrored Merkle root. The frozen
`validatePaymasterUserOp` requires `proof.merkleTreeRoot == _merkleRoot` (latest root only),
and `CreditPool.deposit` pushes a new root on every Bootstrap.

Two phenomena are measured separately and never pooled:

- **D2-A — root contention / validity race.** A Spend proof valid against root R fails once an
  unrelated Bootstrap mirrors R'.
- **D2-B — Bootstrap gas scaling.** `CreditPool.deposit` gas grows with the LeanIMT, against a
  fixed sponsored `callGasLimit` and fixed Paymaster cost caps.

## 2. Actors and who can observe what

| Actor | Role in D2 | Sees |
|---|---|---|
| honest credit issuers (`contender`, `member`) | announce + Bootstrap; every root change is one of their deposits | their own operations; the public chain |
| honest spender(s) (`member`) | hold a credit, prove and spend | own witness, proof timing, own submissions; the public chain |
| bundler (`privgas-minibundler-v1+d2-staged`, operated by us) | receives, simulates, bundles, submits | pending UserOperations, simulation results, its own queue (**A2**) |
| sponsor / Paymaster operator | funds both B3 Paymasters' EntryPoint deposits | the public chain; its deposit balance |
| optional adversary (**A2**) | the bundler operator, or a party fed its simulation results | what the bundler sees; **no public mempool exists in this harness** |

Client-private proof-generation state (witness, proving time, retry lineage before
resubmission) is never treated as public: every recorded field carries a tier
(`records.py`), and the A0 projection of an attempt record contains no client or bundler
field (tested).

## 3. What the harness does, and what is modelled

**FACT (real):** frozen contracts, real signed transactions, real Semaphore v4 / Groth16
proofs verified on chain by B3's own `SemaphoreVerifier`, real `handleOps` simulation
(`eth_call` from the bundler EOA) and real bundle transactions; every root change is a real
Bootstrap UserOperation executing `CreditPool.deposit`.

**Modelled (controlled, not measured):** the *timing*. Stage durations are virtual seconds
(`Segments`), and root-changing arrivals are executed on chain at their virtual position:

```
t0 root observed → t1 prove begins → t2 prove complete → t3 bundler receives
→ t4 simulation begins → t5 simulation result → t6 queued → t7 bundle submitted
→ t8 on-chain validation → t9 inclusion / failure
```

T_prove = t2−t0, T_submit = t3−t2, T_sim = t4−t3, T_queue = t7−t5, T_chain = t8−t7. The
witness is fixed at t0, so a deposit during proving already makes the proof stale. Arrivals
in (t7, t8) are mined in blocks before the bundle — a modelling assumption about ordering,
recorded as such. Failure classification is computed from the root timeline and
cross-checked against the observed revert; a disagreement is counted (`consistent = false`).

## 4. Experiment 1 — zero-contention control (T1)

**FACT.** 50/50 Spends over N ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32} and 5 seeds: proof generated, simulation
accepted, included, execution succeeded. No failures, no inconsistent classifications. The
control therefore does not mask the contention results.

## 5. Experiment 2 / 14 — the deterministic race (T2)

Sequence: observe R → prove (real, verified off chain) → simulate successfully → execute one
unrelated Bootstrap → submit the *unchanged* operation.

**FACT (15/15 runs, N ∈ {8, 16, 32}, 5 seeds).** The Spend fails **on chain**, not at
simulation:

- EntryPoint `FailedOpWithRevert`, reason `AA33 reverted`, inner
  `RootMismatch(proofRoot, storedRoot)`; the reverted proof root equals R and the stored root
  equals the root the intervening Bootstrap produced, in all 15 runs;
- confirmed independently by a `debug_traceTransaction` call trace of the **mined**
  transaction (15/15), not only by a replay;
- **the bundler pays**: the reverted bundle transaction costs ≈ 75.9k gas
  (≈ 152,000 gwei at 2 gwei) and earns no compensation. The CreditPaymaster deposit is
  unchanged, the sender's ETH balance and EntryPoint deposit are unchanged, the sender's
  nonce does not advance, and the nullifier is **not** spent;
- the inverse control (no intervening deposit) is included 15/15;
- re-simulating immediately before submission (P1) drops the operation 15/15 instead, with no
  bundle transaction and no gas loss;
- after re-proving against the new root the same operation is included 15/15. The
  userOpHash excludes the proof, so the account signature is **unchanged** on a retry
  (checked); the only client work is a new proof.

This is the answer to the primary D2 question: **yes, and the frozen architecture fails in
the worst place — after simulation, on chain, at the bundler's expense.**

## 6. Experiment 3 — stochastic contention (T4, T5)

Matrix: N ∈ {8, 16, 32} × λ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2} /s × T ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 5} s × 5 seeds
× 8 trials = 3,000 single-attempt (P0) trials; 75 cells, 120 trials each.

**FACT.** Failure classes: 774 `STALE_BEFORE_SUBMISSION`, 58 `STALE_BEFORE_SIMULATION`,
446 `VALID_AT_SIMULATION_STALE_BEFORE_INCLUSION`, 0 other validation failures, 0 execution
failures, 0 capacity exhaustions, **0 inconsistent classifications**. Every λ = 0 cell has
0/120 failures.

**FACT.** The measured stale-root probability tracks the analytical Poisson baseline
`1 − exp(−λT)`: 70 of 75 cells' Wilson intervals contain it (4 cells with exact binomial
p < 0.05, against ≈ 3.75 expected by chance at 75 cells). The complementary log-log fit gives
slope b1 = 0.977 ± 0.038 on log λT (baseline 1) and intercept −0.001 ± 0.170 (baseline 0);
the likelihood-ratio test against the fixed Poisson baseline does not reject it
(χ²₂ = 1.38, p = 0.50).

**NEGATIVE RESULT.** Pool size has no detectable effect (b2 = −0.008 ± 0.042, LR χ²₁ = 0.04,
p = 0.84; stale rates 421/800, 432/800, 425/800 for N = 8, 16, 32). Root contention is a
function of λ·T alone, not of tree size, credit-set size or proof depth.

**INFERENCE.** Under this arrival model the empirical agreement is close to tautological: any
root change inside the window invalidates the proof, so the failure probability *is* the
Poisson no-arrival probability. The experimental content is not the curve but its
preconditions, which were not assumed: every root change really does invalidate (no
tolerance, no history), a change never fails to invalidate, and nothing else failed in 3,000
trials.

**FACT (of note).** 446 of 1,278 stale failures (35%) passed simulation and failed on chain —
the simulation→inclusion class specifically, under the default segment profile (30% of the
window before simulation, 60% after).

## 7. Experiment 12 — which segment dominates (T7)

One segment set to 2 s, the others to 0.05 s, λ = 1/s.

**FACT.** Total stale probability is indistinguishable across profiles (0.80–0.90, all
consistent with 1 − exp(−λT) = 0.889): *where* the exposure sits does not change *whether*
the operation goes stale.

**FACT.** It changes entirely **where the failure is detected and who pays**:

| dominant segment | P0 outcome | P1 outcome |
|---|---|---|
| prove / submit / sim | rejected at simulation, bundler loses nothing | identical |
| queue (t5→t7) | 27/40 fail **on chain**, ≈ 4.1 M gwei bundler loss over 40 trials | 27/40 dropped at re-simulation, **0 gwei** lost |
| chain (t7→t8) | 23/40 fail on chain | 23/40 still fail on chain (P1 catches only 4) |

**INFERENCE.** The window decomposes into a *recoverable* part (everything before the bundle
transaction is signed, which re-simulation converts into a cheap rejection) and an
*irreducible* part T_chain, between submission and on-chain validation, which no bundler-side
policy in this experiment can close. A defense that only re-simulates addresses the first
part; the second is where the architecture itself is exposed.

## 8. Experiments 15 / 16 — bundler policies and the client retry loop (T6)

N = 16, λ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}, T ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5} s, policies P0/P1/P2, 5 seeds × 4 trials,
retry cap 10 attempts; the same arrival stream is replayed for all three policies (paired).

**FACT (pooled over the 15 cells with λ > 0, 320 trials per policy; identical arrival
streams, so the first-attempt stale rate is 0.617 for all three).** Re-simulation does not buy
liveness — it buys cost and detection:

| | P0 | P1 | P2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| successes within 10 attempts | 242/320 | 241/320 | 238/320 |
| attempts (= proofs) per success | 5.63 | 5.71 | 5.85 |
| simulations per success | 4.80 | 6.99 | 5.80 |
| failures rejected at simulation | 863 | 866 | 874 |
| failures dropped at re-simulation | 0 | 185 | 194 |
| failures **on chain** | 257 | 85 | 86 |
| total bundler loss (gwei) | 38.6 M | 12.9 M | 13.1 M |
| mean p95 time to success (s) | 8.00 | 7.11 | 6.50 |

P1 and P2 cut on-chain failures and bundler gas loss by ≈ 67% for the same success rate. P2
reaches P1's benefit with ≈ 1.2 fewer simulations per success (it re-simulates only after an
observed root-changing transaction) and detects the failure at the moment of the change
rather than at submission time, which makes it the best of the three on latency: mean p95
time-to-success 1.50 s better than P0 (better in 13 of 15 cells) against P1's 0.89 s (10 of
15).

The 85–86 on-chain failures that survive P1/P2 are exactly the T_chain residue of §7.

**FACT.** Client cost is dominated by re-proving: the retry loop needs one fresh proof per
attempt (the signature and userOpHash are unchanged), so proofs per success equals attempts
per success. Wasted proving time grows with λ·T, and at λ·T ≥ 2 the 10-attempt cap starts to
bite (see the generated table for per-cell values).

**FACT — who pays for contention.** The user pays **no ETH** for a failed attempt in any
policy: the sender is sponsored, the failed bundle reverts before any charge, and neither the
CreditPaymaster deposit nor the sender's balance moves. The **sponsor** pays exactly one
Spend charge per delivered Spend (≈ 744,900 gwei, invariant across λ, T and policy: a failed
Spend never reaches `postOp` accounting) plus, separately, the Bootstrap charge of every
root-changing deposit (≈ 8.68 M gwei of sponsor money across the λ > 0 policy cells — those
deposits are other users' credits, not overhead of this Spend). The **bundler** pays the
reverted bundle gas: up to ≈ 1.03 M gas per delivered Spend in the worst cell under P0. The
**client** pays CPU only.

**HYPOTHESIS (not tested here).** In a deployment the relevant λ is the credit-issuance rate
of the whole pool, so contention cost per Spend should grow with adoption while the anonymity
set does too — a protocol that ties the anonymity set to a single mutable root couples the two.
Testing that needs a deployment-derived issuance rate, which this pilot does not provide.

## 9. Retry loop with measured proof time (T8)

Here the proof segment is the **real measured** proof time of each attempt (mean 0.288 s,
max 0.459 s at these depths); T_submit = T_sim = 0.05 s, T_queue = 0, and T_chain is an
assumed block interval.

**FACT / INFERENCE (block interval is an assumption, the rest is measured).** With a 2 s block
interval, λ = 0.25 /s gives 10/10 successes within 10 attempts (0.4 retries per success) and
λ = 1 /s gives 6/10. With a 12 s block interval, λ = 0.25 /s gives 3/10 successes (25 retries
per success among those that succeeded, ≈ 7.1 s of wasted proving per success) and λ = 1 /s
gives **0/10** — no Spend completed within 10 attempts.

**INFERENCE.** Proof generation is not the dominant term at these depths; the block interval
is. A protocol whose proof must name the latest root is bounded by how often that root
changes relative to *inclusion* latency, not proving latency.

## 10. Experiment 18 — concurrent spenders (T9)

**FACT.** Spends do not change the root, so with no intervening Bootstrap all M ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}
concurrent Spends against the same root are included (5 runs each: 5/5, 10/10, 20/20, 40/40).

**FACT.** A single Bootstrap between simulation and inclusion invalidates **every** pending
Spend: 5/5, 10/10, 20/20, 40/40 fail with `RootMismatch`, and the bundler's loss scales with
M (≈ 1.22 M gwei per run at M = 8, ≈ 608k gas of reverted bundles).

**INFERENCE.** Failures are perfectly correlated across users, because they share one piece of
mutable state. This is the amplification property that matters for a defense: the cost of one
root change is proportional to the number of pending proofs, not to 1.

## 11. Experiment 19 — bundle size and mixed bundles (T10)

**FACT.** k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} Spends proven against the same root are all included when no
Bootstrap intervenes. One intervening Bootstrap reverts the **whole bundle**: 0/k included,
for every k. In the `one_stale_rest_fresh` variant (one stale op, k−1 re-proved against the
new root) the bundle still reverts entirely — the EntryPoint's `FailedOp` aborts the whole
`handleOps` call at the first failing operation, so **one stale proof destroys the bundle's
valid operations**.

**FACT (mixed bundles).** `[Bootstrap, Spend(R)]` and `[Spend(R), Bootstrap]` both include
**both** operations, in either order: EntryPoint v0.9.0 validates every operation before it
executes any, so the Spend validates against the pre-bundle root even when the Bootstrap
precedes it in the array. `[Bootstrap, Spend(R')]` and `[Spend(R'), Bootstrap]` — a Spend
proven against the root the bundle itself will produce — fail validation with `RootMismatch`
(failing index 1 and 0 respectively). No protocol change was made to support mixed bundles.

## 12. Experiment 25 — adversarial schedule (A2) (T11)

The only observer this harness supports for a *targeted* schedule is **A2**: the bundler
operator (or a party it feeds), which sees a simulation acceptance. There is no public
mempool here, so an A0/A1 adversary cannot see a pending Spend at all and is not modelled.
Honest λ = 0, so every root change is adversarial; the adversary deposits 0.05 s after a
simulation acceptance.

**FACT.** Every adversarial deposit invalidates every pending Spend: with M = 1 the victim
loses exactly one attempt per deposit; with M = 4 victims, **4 failed attempts per deposit**.
Under P0 each deposit also costs the bundler ≈ 152k gwei of reverted bundle gas; under P1 the
same deposits cost the bundler nothing but still force the same number of re-proofs. With
budget 8 the victim needs 9 proofs and 18 s (p50) instead of 1 proof and 2 s.

**FACT (cost side).** Each root change requires a *fresh announced account*: the attacker
burns the non-refundable fee F = 0.021 ETH (`AnnouncementRegistry`), locks vMin = 0.01 ETH at
its own account (retained), pays the announcement transaction, and the **sponsor** pays the
Bootstrap's gas (≈ 1.05 M gwei per deposit in these runs; ≈ 47 M gwei of sponsor money for
the 40-deposit cells).

**INFERENCE.** Sybil pricing bounds the *targeted* attack: the attacker burns ≈ 0.021 ETH
(less the value of the credit it obtains, ≤ 0.005 ETH of sponsorship) to destroy ≈ 0.00015 ETH
of bundler gas and M re-proofs. Griefing is therefore expensive per unit of damage — but the
same damage is produced *for free* by honest issuers, who pay that fee for their own reasons.
**The realistic threat to liveness is ordinary load, not an attacker.** Note also that the
attack consumes sponsor funds, which the frozen design does not rate-limit per unit time.

## 13. Experiment 13 — real proof-generation latency (T3)

Real `@semaphore-protocol/core` 4.14.2 proofs with the pinned artifacts (depths 2–6; depths
6–7 were pinned for this pilot by first-download sha256 and authenticated on chain), on the
machine recorded in the batch manifest (Apple M1, 8 cores, 8 GB, Node v22.19.0).

**FACT.** Proof generation is 180 ms (p50, group 4 / depth 2) to 266 ms (group 64 / depth 6),
i.e. ≈ +21 ms per depth level; p95 never exceeds 277 ms and the standard deviation is
≤ 11 ms. The first proof at each depth costs 351–457 ms (artifact loading), recorded
separately as the cold sample. Off-chain verification is ≈ 10 ms at every depth. One proof per
pool size was additionally verified **on chain** by the frozen `SemaphoreVerifier` through a
real simulation (5/5 accepted).

**INFERENCE.** At these sizes proving is a few hundred milliseconds — an order of magnitude
below a single block interval — so it is not the dominant term of the vulnerability window
(§9). Artificial delay was used only as an experimental control (§6–§8); no reported proof
latency is synthetic.

## 14. D2-B — Bootstrap gas scaling (T12)

Tree grown one real Bootstrap at a time to 256 members, 2 seeds; identical results for both
seeds at every size.

**FACT — deposit gas grows with the tree.** `CreditPool.deposit` frame gas (call-trace
measured) is 121,763 at tree size 0 and 562,428 at size 255. The cost is governed by the
LeanIMT depth: the worst case at each level (sizes 2^d − 1) rises by ≈ 61,183 gas per level
(1 → 8 `PoseidonT3` delegatecalls), while an insertion that starts a new level is cheap
(≈ 190k at size 128). The Bootstrap UserOperation's `actualGasUsed` at the minimal sufficient
limit rises from 392,166 to 832,831 gas, and the sponsored charge from ≈ 784k to ≈ 1.67 M gwei.

**FACT — the frozen Bootstrap `callGasLimit` (160,000) covers only tree size 0.** At every
size ≥ 1 of 256 tested, the Bootstrap is *accepted by the Paymaster, included, and its
execution reverts out of gas*. The minimal sufficient `callGasLimit` is 130,077 at size 0,
181,347 at size 1 and 584,667 at size 255.

**FACT — the failure is not free and not retryable.** `BootstrapPaymaster` marks the account
used during validation, so a failed insertion **consumes the account's one-time sponsored
grant** (`isUsed = true`) while `hasDeposited` stays false, and the sponsor is charged
≈ 843k gwei for the failed attempt. In the natural sequence (every issuer using the frozen
limit, no high-limit help) the tree stays at size 1 forever: attempts 1–5 all fail identically
and each burns one issuer's admission and one sponsored grant.

**FACT — fee-cap interaction (T12, third table).** With the W1 gas limits, the *frozen*
Bootstrap is refused by `BootstrapPaymaster.MaxCostExceeded` at maxFeePerGas ≥ 9 gwei, and the
Spend by `CreditPaymaster.MaxCostExceeded` at ≥ 8 gwei — both **below B3's own advertised
10 gwei cap** (`MAX_ACCEPTED_MAX_FEE_PER_GAS`), at every tree size. The analytic envelope
p_max = 0.005 ETH / Σ(gas limits) matches every simulated verdict. Tree growth tightens it
further but only slightly (8.48 → 8.21 gwei from size 1 to 128 at the minimal sufficient
limit), because the sum is dominated by the fixed verification limits; the experimental
1.5 M limit collapses it to 2.6 gwei.

**INFERENCE.** The binding constraint is the *fixed wei cost cap* against *growing gas*, not
the cap on `callGasLimit + verificationGasLimit` (`MAX_CREDIT_GAS` = 500,000, which the Spend
respects). Raising the call-gas limit to keep deposits working directly shrinks the gas-price
range in which the protocol can operate at all: the two frozen parameters trade against each
other, and B3's sybil-resistance invariant (F > κ·c_credit·p_max) is stated against the very
cap that becomes unreachable.

## 15. Stop conditions

None fired. Specifically: the deterministic race reproduces (15/15); proof generation never
used a root different from the recorded one (checked on every attempt, raises otherwise);
simulation and inclusion are separable in this harness (that is what `StagedBundler` does, and
the 446 simulation→inclusion failures show it); retries never modify B3 semantics (same
contracts, same op, new proof); no failure is discarded (every attempt is a record; capacity
exhaustion would be recorded as `HARNESS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — it never occurred); the
experimental Bootstrap limits are recorded separately from the frozen value everywhere and
are tested to differ from it; measured stale probability is not zero at large λ·T.

One harness defect was found and fixed **during** the pilot: in policy P2 the arrival window
was popped in one batch, so deposits later than the drop time were applied to the chain even
though the retry restarted earlier in virtual time, producing 37 inconsistent P2 records out
of 4,349. `ArrivalProcess.pop_next` now pops one arrival at a time and P2 stops at the drop;
the policy experiment was re-run from scratch with the fix, and a regression test
(`test_pop_next_leaves_later_arrivals_pending`) pins it. After the re-run, **all 11,690
attempt records of the batch classify consistently** (0 inconsistent in every experiment).

## 16. Assumptions and limitations

1. **Timing is modelled, not measured.** λ and the segment durations are experimental
   controls; the only measured latencies are proof generation, simulation, submission wall
   time and (in §9) the block interval as an explicit assumption. Nothing here estimates the
   real root-update rate of a deployed system — that rate is the parameter a deployment would
   have to supply.
2. **One bundler, ours.** `privgas-minibundler-v1+d2-staged` enforces no ERC-7562 rules, has
   no public mempool, no reputation, no fee replacement, and does not drop a failing operation
   and re-bundle the rest (a real bundler would, which would reduce the bundle-poisoning cost
   in §11 but not the per-operation failure). No ERC-7562 or production-bundler claim follows.
3. **Ordering assumption.** Arrivals scheduled inside (t7, t8) are mined before the bundle.
   Real inclusion ordering is a builder's choice; the alternative ordering would move those
   failures into the next attempt, not remove them.
4. **Proof cache.** Identical proof inputs reuse one real proof within a chain; attempts carry
   that proof's measured time. Proofs-per-success counts attempts (what a real client pays),
   not cache misses.
5. **Experimental Bootstrap `callGasLimit`** (1,000,000 in D2-A, 1,500,000 in D2-B growth) is
   a wallet-side parameter needed to build a tree at all. It is never the frozen baseline, and
   §14 measures the frozen value separately. Because the frozen limit cannot build a tree,
   **D2-A's contention experiments presuppose the D2-B fix**; on frozen parameters alone the
   pool never exceeds one member and root contention cannot arise.
6. **Single chain configuration:** `b3_compat_local`, automine (one transaction per block),
   base fee pinned to 1 gwei, W1 gas limits (verification 300,000), single-op bundle
   preVerificationGas calibration reused for multi-op bundles (which is why bundler net is
   positive for large k in T10).
7. **No D2-B result depends on proofs**, and no D2-A result depends on the announcement or
   asset-delivery steps; both reuse the unchanged W1 workflow.

## 17. Unexpected findings

1. **Retry needs no re-signing.** The v0.9.0 paymaster-signature suffix excludes the proof
   from `userOpHash`, so a stale Spend is repaired by swapping proof bytes; the account
   signature, nonce and every gas field stay identical (measured, 15/15).
2. **The bundler is the only party that loses money** on a stale-root failure; the sponsor and
   the user lose nothing on chain. The incentive to re-simulate therefore sits with the party
   that cannot fix the protocol.
3. **A Bootstrap and a Spend proven against the *old* root coexist happily in one bundle**
   (either order), because validation precedes execution — but a Spend proven against the root
   that bundle creates cannot.
4. **A failed Bootstrap consumes the sponsored grant permanently** (D2-B), which turns a gas
   mis-parameterisation into a per-account, non-recoverable liveness failure and a wasted
   admission fee.
5. **B3's advertised 10 gwei fee cap is unreachable** for both sponsored operations at the
   measured gas limits (8.8 gwei for Bootstrap, 6.2 for Spend).

## 18. Decision: which D2 case applies

**CASE D — both matter**, with the two failure modes independent:

- Root contention is real, reaches the worst stage (valid at simulation, invalid at
  inclusion, 446/3,000 trials under the default profile), scales predictably as
  1 − exp(−λ·T) with no pool-size effect, is perfectly correlated across concurrent users and
  across a bundle, and imposes measurable re-proving and bundler-gas cost. Bundler-side
  re-simulation removes the on-chain cost of the pre-submission part but cannot touch T_chain.
- Gas scaling is a separate, deterministic failure: the frozen Bootstrap parameters cannot
  build a credit pool beyond one member, each failure permanently consumes a sponsored grant,
  and fixing it by raising the call-gas limit shrinks an already-unreachable fee envelope.

**Does D2 survive falsification?** Yes, for both parts, with the explicit caveat that the
contention *rate* is an assumption of the experiment rather than a measurement of a deployed
system, and that D2-A presupposes D2-B's parameter fix.

## 19. Recommended next research action (not implemented)

Characterise the **irreducible** part of the window before designing anything: measure
whether the simulation→inclusion exposure T_chain, which no bundler policy closed here,
admits a protocol-side answer that is not simply "retain historical roots" — and, in the same
step, establish whether the gas-scaling failure is B3-specific or general to
mirror-latest-state Paymasters under ERC-7562's storage rules. The D2 kill condition
(`docs/research-plan.md` §3) explicitly aims at the first question: if bounded root history
plus routine staking removes the problem at realistic load with no significant tradeoff, D2 is
engineering rather than research, and the tradeoff to measure is anonymity-set / state-growth
versus liveness. That comparison requires a defense that has not been designed and is
deliberately not attempted here.
